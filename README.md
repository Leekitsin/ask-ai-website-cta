# Ask AI Website CTA

A simple marketing experiment exploring how pre-filled AI prompts can turn website CTAs into AI-assisted discovery conversations.

## The Idea

Most business websites follow a familiar journey:

**Visitor → Website → Browse → Product/Service → Contact**

But there is another possibility:

**Visitor → Website → Ask AI → Conversation → Discovery**

Instead of asking visitors to navigate through multiple product and service pages, an **Ask AI** CTA can give them a simple way to start a conversation with an AI assistant.

The AI is given context about the business and its offerings, then asks the visitor a simple question:

> **"What are you interested in?"**

From there, the conversation can adapt to what the visitor is actually looking for.

---

## How It Works

The concept uses pre-filled prompts in links to AI platforms such as:

- ChatGPT
- Claude
- Google Gemini

When a visitor clicks an AI button, the selected AI platform opens with a prompt already prepared.

The prompt:

1. Introduces the business and what it does.
2. Explains its broad range of products, solutions and services.
3. Positions the business as a solution partner rather than simply a product seller.
4. Asks the visitor one simple question: **"What are you interested in?"**
5. Uses the visitor's response to continue the discovery conversation.

## Example Use Case

This project uses a **generic technology solutions company** as the example.

For example, a business might offer:

- Hardware
- Software
- Cloud and productivity solutions
- Business technology
- Technology procurement
- Deployment and implementation
- Professional services
- Managed services
- Device and IT lifecycle management
- Ongoing support

The same approach can be adapted to almost any business with multiple products, services or solutions.

## Make It Your Own

This project is designed as a **reusable template**.

If you want to implement the concept for your own company, replace the generic business information in the prompt with information about your organisation.

You can customise:

- Company name
- What your company does
- Products and services
- Industries or audiences you serve
- Your value proposition
- Areas of expertise
- The type of discovery question you want to ask

### Example

Instead of:

> "Tell me about this company as a technology partner for businesses."

You could use:

> "Tell me about [COMPANY NAME] as a [TYPE OF BUSINESS] that helps [TARGET AUDIENCE] with [KEY SOLUTIONS]."

Then provide the AI with enough accurate information about your business to have a useful conversation with visitors.

**Tip:** Keep the first interaction simple. You don't need to ask visitors for their industry, company size, budget and technology environment immediately. Give them useful context first, then let the conversation develop naturally.

## Why This Is Interesting for Marketers

The goal isn't to build another website chatbot.

The idea is to use AI platforms that people already use and create a new type of website CTA that can start a discovery conversation.

Traditional CTA:

**"Contact Us"**

AI-assisted CTA:

**"Ask AI"**

This could be particularly useful for businesses with complex offerings where visitors may know the problem they have but don't know which product, service or solution they need.

Potential applications include:

- B2B technology companies
- SaaS companies
- Consulting firms
- Professional services
- IT service providers
- Agencies
- Financial services
- Education businesses
- Healthcare organisations
- Other businesses with complex product or service portfolios

## Important Considerations

This is a **proof of concept and marketing experiment**, not a production AI application.

AI platforms may change how URL-based prompt pre-filling works, so links should be tested before being deployed on a live website.

The AI's response is also not controlled by the website. The quality of the conversation depends on the information provided in the prompt, the AI platform being used, and the visitor's interaction with it.

Always provide accurate company information and avoid asking the AI to invent products, services, partnerships or capabilities.
